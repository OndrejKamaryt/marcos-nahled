What we propose

We recommend a premium, grounded hybrid athlete brand built around you. It keeps the physical intensity of your content and gives coaching its own page that explains how it works and how to apply.

  • Training and coaching are the main offer today, so the website's first job is to show who you help, what you offer and what to do next.
  • We suggest your name as the common thread across everything you do.
  • Your other projects get their own path when you are ready. Nothing about them goes public until you decide.

This is a recommendation. You can change any part of it in your answers.

What we keep from your public profiles

We started from what you already show in public, because people who follow you should recognise you on the new website.

  • The look people know you for: dark gyms, strength work and outdoor running, in muted colour with close crops.
  • Three programmes as the backbone of the site: Hybrid Base Builder, Hyrox Prep System and Hybrid Peak Protocol, Levels 1 to 3, as sold on Payhip.
  • Your name as Marcos Pier, and the MP monogram from your coaching page if you want to keep it. Questions 4 and 7 ask.

We looked at public pages only, so tell us if anything here is out of date.

Your first website

We propose a small first release that does one job well: a visitor sees quickly who you help, what you offer and what to do next.

Pages

  • Home
  • Programmes: an overview with a clear comparison, and one page for each programme
  • Coaching: how it works and how to apply
  • About
  • Contact

Homepage order

  1. 1An opening with you, a specific description of your offer and one main training button.
  2. 2A short section on your background, using the qualifications, experience and results you confirm.
  3. 3Training options, with downloadable programmes and personal coaching shown separately.
  4. 4How coaching works, followed by client stories and outcomes you approve.
  5. 5A short personal story with an approachable portrait.
  6. 6A small space for your other projects, only if you want them public.
  7. 7A small selection of useful content and a final button for the next step.

Programmes and coaching

We suggest keeping these clearly apart. A programme is a PDF that people buy and download. Coaching is personal, and we suggest it starts with an application. Each programme page covers who it suits, level, duration, weekly commitment, equipment, format, what is included, support and price.

Payhip

We suggest keeping Payhip for checkout and delivery at first, if that suits your current setup. The website explains and compares the programmes, then sends buyers to the right Payhip page, so your shop does not need rebuilding for the first release.

Content

A compact content section with a few useful pieces takes the place of a blog, so nothing sits empty.

Three websites we would learn from

We would take specific ideas from these, not their look.

  • Nick Bare (opens in a new tab)

    one person clearly at the centre, with a separate route and a single button for each offer.

  • OMNIA Performance (opens in a new tab)

    the page order of who it is for, the service, proof and one next step, plus a short way to find the right plan, with you kept at the centre.

  • Tracksmith (opens in a new tab)

    restraint in storytelling and photography, with few words, generous space and a quiet way in for people new to you.
